# Legal drafts (for your lawyer, not ready to sign)

These are working drafts written from how Backroute actually works, so your lawyer starts from the real facts instead
of a blank page. **They are not legal advice and must be reviewed by a licensed attorney before any carrier, driver or
customer sees them.** Words in [brackets] are yours to fill in.

| File | What it's for | Who agrees to it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `carrier-services-agreement.md` | The contract with each carrier, including the authority for the AI to act and sign for them | The carrier's owner, at sign-up |
| `terms-of-service.md` | Using the app and website | Everyone who signs in |
| `privacy-policy.md` | What data is collected and why | Published on the site |
| `driver-text-consent.md` | Drivers agreeing to texts and calls from the dispatch number | Each driver, when added |
| `call-recording-and-ai-notice.md` | What the AI says on calls, and when calls may be recorded | Built into every call |

## Questions for counsel

1. **Dispatch service, not a broker.** Backroute finds and negotiates loads for carriers and is paid by the carrier, not
   the shipper. Confirm this doesn't need broker authority (49 U.S.C. §13904) in how it's structured, marketed and
   billed, including any percentage-of-freight fee and any partner referral fees (factoring, insurance).
2. **Signing for the carrier.** The AI signs rate cons (a signature page, or in DocuSign and brokers' portals) in the
   name of a person the owner authorizes. Confirm the agency language in the carrier agreement, and that it satisfies
   E-SIGN and UETA for these documents.
3. **Accounts and passwords.** The AI signs in to, and can open, accounts on carrier setup networks (MyCarrierPackets,
   RMIS, Highway) and scheduling sites for the carrier, and stores those passwords encrypted. Check those sites' terms
   on automated access and agents acting for a carrier.
4. **Texts and calls (TCPA, CTIA).** The consent wording, keeping proof of consent, STOP handling, and quiet hours.
5. **AI disclosure and recording.** The AI says it's an AI at the start of every call. Some states require all parties'
   consent to record (California, Florida, Illinois, Maryland, Massachusetts, Montana, New Hampshire, Pennsylvania,
   Washington and others) and some require bot disclosure (California). Confirm the notice and where recording is off.
6. **Liability.** A cap tied to fees paid, no liability for the carrier's safety, compliance or cargo, and what happens
   when the AI makes a mistake (a wrong booking, a missed appointment). Consider errors-and-omissions insurance.
7. **Data.** Driver locations and hours from the ELD, and what drivers must be told. Data kept after an account closes.
